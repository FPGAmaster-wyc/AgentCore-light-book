AgentCore-Light is a desktop device that shows what your computer is doing through three coloured lights and a buzzer. It connects over Bluetooth or over a USB cable. The desktop app lets you drive the lights and buzzer by hand, and it can also connect to your AI tools so the device shows working, done, error or waiting-for-confirmation states on its own.

#1. Status at a glance

LightMeaningWhat to do
All LEDs flashThe device is powering onWait for it to enter a connection mode
Red blinkingBluetooth mode, waiting for the computer to connectPair over Bluetooth in Windows and in the AgentCore-Light app
Yellow blinkingUSB serial mode, waiting for the app to connectPlug in the USB cable and select Start USB mode in the app
Green breathingThe current connection is up; the device is idle and standing byYou can start using the app, light control, or your AI tools
All LEDs offThe device is powered offPress and hold the button for 3 seconds to power on

“Green breathing” means the green light fading slowly up and down. It means the device is connected and standing by — it is not blinking, and it is not a fault.

The red, yellow and green lights show working status. The charge and full indicators only light up while USB power is connected; on battery both are off, to save power.

#2. Getting started

#2.1 Before you start

  1. Charge the device, or make sure it has enough battery.
  2. Unzip the downloaded AgentCore-Light.zip to get AgentCore-Light.exe.
  3. For a USB connection, use a Type-C cable that supports data transfer.
  4. For a Bluetooth connection, make sure your computer has Bluetooth and that it is switched on.

Switch the app to English first. The app starts up in Chinese. Open Settings (top right) → Language → English. Every button name in this manual is the one you will see after that — for example Start Bluetooth mode and Start USB mode. Your choice is remembered on this computer.

The first time you run AgentCore-Light.exe, Windows may show “Windows protected your PC”, or your antivirus may warn you. This is normal for software without a code-signing certificate — the software is not at fault. See 2.2 for what to do.

#2.2 What to do when your system says “not safe”

AgentCore-Light is made by an independent developer. It has no paid code-signing certificate and no publisher verification, so Windows or your antivirus may block it the first time you run it — that is caused by the missing signature, not by a virus in the software.

Why does it get blocked?

  • Windows SmartScreen mainly looks at how many people have downloaded a file. A brand-new release has few downloads, so it gets tagged as an “unrecognised app”. That has nothing to do with whether the file is safe.
  • Antivirus software is generally cautious about programs that are both unsigned and not widely used. Blocking or quarantining them is a common default — it would rather be wrong than sorry.

What the software actually does:

  • It runs entirely on your own machine. The console page listens on 127.0.0.1 only, so only this computer can open it — other devices on the same network cannot reach it.
  • It sends no data anywhere, apart from checking for software updates.
  • Start-at-sign-in is written under your own user account, needs no administrator rights, and changes neither system settings nor other software.

What to do, by the kind of prompt you get:

① The blue window, “Windows protected your PC”

This is Windows SmartScreen. Click More info in the window; a Run anyway button appears — click it to start the program.

② Your browser says “isn’t commonly downloaded”

In your browser’s download list, find the archive you just downloaded and choose Keep (in Edge: “Keep anyway”).

③ Your antivirus flags it, or the file has been quarantined or deleted

First get the file back:

  • Open Windows Security → Virus & threat protection → Protection history, find the AgentCore-Light.exe entry, click Actions, and choose Allow or Restore.

Then add it to the trusted list so it is not blocked every time:

  • Windows Security → Virus & threat protection → Manage settings (under “Virus & threat protection settings”) → scroll to “Exclusions” → Add or remove exclusions → Add → File, then select AgentCore-Light.exe.
  • Third-party antivirus (360 Total Security, Huorong, Tencent PC Manager, and similar): add the file under “Trusted / Allow list / Exclusions”, or choose “Restore and trust” on the warning pop-up.

It is worth adding AgentCore-Light.exe to your antivirus exclusions right after downloading, before the first run — that saves being blocked again and again later.

Still not comfortable after running it?

  • You can upload AgentCore-Light.exe to https://www.virustotal.com/ and have seventy-odd engines scan it at once. Unsigned software from individual developers often draws one or two hits; as long as it is not a broad detection, those are false positives.
  • To see what the software does on the network, watch the event stream in the console: it only talks to the device you connected over USB or Bluetooth.

#2.3 Powering on

  1. Press and hold the button on the device for about 3 seconds.
  2. Once the time is up, the device immediately plays its power-on light effect.
  3. It then enters Bluetooth mode by default; red blinking means it is waiting for the computer to connect.

#2.4 Powering off

Press and hold the button for about 3 seconds. When the time is up, the device switches its status lights off immediately and powers down to a low-power state.

Plugging in USB for charging or for USB communication does not power the device on. When you want to use it, you still need to press and hold the button for 3 seconds.

#3. Connecting over Bluetooth (recommended for everyday wireless use)

Bluetooth mode suits a device sitting on your desk where you would rather not have cables.

#3.1 Pairing in Windows

  1. After powering on, check that the red light is blinking.
  2. Open Windows Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Add device → Bluetooth.
  3. Select AgentCore-Light in the list.
  4. Follow the Windows prompts to pair.
  5. On the device details page, click Connect until Windows shows it as connected.
  6. The device shows green breathing, which means the Bluetooth connection is ready.

Once paired, you normally do not need to pair again.

#3.2 Connecting in AgentCore-Light

  1. Double-click AgentCore-Light.exe to run it.
  2. A browser page opens automatically for configuration. If it does not, open http://127.0.0.1:18793/ yourself.
  3. Click Start Bluetooth mode.
  4. Wait for the app to show the device as connected.

In Bluetooth mode Windows may show the device as an audio device. That is so Windows manages the classic Bluetooth connection reliably; the actual light and buzzer control runs over the Bluetooth data channel and will not play your computer’s sound.

#4. Connecting over USB serial

USB serial mode suits a wired connection, or a computer without Bluetooth.

#4.1 Switching to USB mode

  1. Make sure the device is powered on.
  2. Press the button once (short press).
  3. The yellow light starts blinking, which means the device has switched to USB serial mode and is waiting for the app to connect.

#4.2 Connecting on the computer

  1. Connect the device to the computer with a Type-C cable that supports data transfer.
  2. Open AgentCore-Light.exe.
  3. A browser page opens automatically for configuration. If it does not, open http://127.0.0.1:18793/ yourself.
  4. Click Start USB mode.
  5. Wait for the app to connect; green breathing on the device means you are ready to go.

If the app stops the USB serial service, the device goes back to yellow blinking and waits for the next connection.

#5. Switching between the two connection modes

The device starts in Bluetooth mode. Each short press of the button switches between the two:

text
Bluetooth mode (red blinking)
        ⇅ short press
USB serial mode (yellow blinking)

When you switch modes the device turns the other transport off, which reduces power draw and avoids the confusion of two channels being connected at once.

Selecting Start Bluetooth mode or Start USB mode in the app also closes the other connection automatically.

#6. Basic app operation

Once the device is connected, you can do all of the following in the AgentCore-Light console.

#6.1 Testing the lights by hand

In the Light control area you can test the red, yellow and green lights — steady on or blinking — and run All off, All on and All blink tests.

#6.2 Adjusting brightness

The default brightness is 30%, a balance between desk presence and battery life.

To go brighter:

  1. Find the Brightness slider in the console.
  2. Drag it right to brighten, left to dim.
  3. As soon as you let go, the setting is sent to the device and saved on this computer; it is restored automatically the next time the app starts or the device reconnects.

#6.3 Buzzer

You can turn the buzzer on or off in the console and set its volume. In a quiet room, start with a low volume while you test.

#6.4 Battery

Once the device is connected, the console shows:

  • the current battery percentage;
  • battery voltage;
  • whether USB power is connected;
  • when the status was last updated.

While USB is connected: the charge indicator lights up until the battery is full, then the full indicator lights up. Unplug USB and both charging indicators go out.

#6.5 Quitting the app

There are two ways to shut the app down completely (for instance when you want this computer to stop driving the light), and either will do:

  1. Quit from the console (recommended): click Quit in the top right and confirm. Both the interface and the background service stop, and you can close the console page in your browser.
  2. Double-click the quit script: in the app’s folder (or in the package your seller gave you), double-click 退出AgentCore-Light.cmd. Use this one when the console will not open or you cannot find the Quit button.

Neither way turns off start-at-sign-in: the app still launches the next time you sign in. To stop that too, switch Start at sign-in off in the console’s top bar.

To use it again, double-click the AgentCore-Light icon on your desktop.

#7. Connecting your AI tools

To have AI working status shown on the device automatically:

  1. First connect the device as described in chapter 3 or 4, and confirm green breathing.
  2. In the app, open Connect AI tools.
  3. Follow the on-screen prompts to select and install the AI tool integrations you need.
  4. Restart the relevant AI tool, or start a new session, as prompted.
  5. Once the AI tool starts working, the device shows the matching light effect — for example working, done, error, or waiting for confirmation.

#A note on Doubao

The Doubao desktop app has no hook to attach to, so it uses a built-in listener: just install Doubao under Connect AI tools — no need to restart Doubao, and nothing extra to install.

After that, use Doubao normally:

  1. You send a question → the light shows Working;
  2. Doubao finishes answering → the light gives one Done signal, then returns to standby.

A few things worth knowing:

  • Windows and macOS desktop versions of Doubao are supported. On Linux Doubao is installed in several different ways (some distributions have a native package, some run it under Wine); we try to support those too.
  • If you have Doubao installed but the light does not respond, send us the app’s logs — the app writes a line every 5 seconds saying whether it found Doubao’s log, so the problem is easy to spot.
  • The app works by reading two “request records” that Doubao itself writes to its run log (one when you send a question, one when the answer completes). It does not take screenshots, does not read your chat content, and does not log keystrokes; the whole judgement runs locally.
  • Doubao can be minimised or covered by another window; the answer still triggers the light. You can minimise Doubao and do something else as soon as you have sent your question.
  • Only two moments are recognised today — “question sent” and “answer finished”. Intermediate states such as Doubao asking you to confirm something are not reported separately.
  • If a major Doubao update breaks the detection, contact us for an updated app (the app keeps self-check logs that show at a glance whether Doubao changed).

If you do not need the AI integration for now, you can simply use the app’s manual light and buzzer controls.

#8. Standby, sleep and auto power-off

#8.1 The idle breathing light

Once an AI tool is connected, the light is handed over to the app: with nothing running, the green light shows low-brightness breathing, a little dimmer than the normal working brightness, which is also a power-saving measure. Right after connecting, before the app has taken over the light, the green light breathes in the same way — seeing it fade slowly up and down means the connection is ready.

#8.2 Going to sleep after a while idle

After 10 minutes continuously idle (the default), the device turns all status lights off and goes to sleep, saving more power. The connection stays up while it sleeps; when your AI tool has new work the light comes back on by itself, with no button press needed.

#8.3 Auto power-off when Bluetooth drops

In Bluetooth mode, if there is no connection for 5 minutes continuously (the computer shut down, Bluetooth was switched off, or the app quit), the device powers itself off; this happens even while USB charging is plugged in. When you want to use it again, press and hold the button for about 3 seconds to power on.

Both of those times can be changed: idle sleep defaults to 10 minutes and disconnect power-off to 5 minutes, both in the range 0–3600 seconds, where 0 turns the automatic action off. Developers can change them with the API commands IDLE_SLEEP_SEC: and BT_DISCONNECT_OFF_SEC: (see section 15 of the API manual); everyone else can contact support.

#8.4 Charging and battery care

  • You can charge the device from any ordinary USB power source.
  • When you are not using it, press and hold the button to power it off — that gives the lowest standby drain.
  • If you will not use it for a long time, top the battery up now and then so it is not left deeply discharged for months.

#9. Troubleshooting

#My antivirus says this software is a virus

It is almost always a false positive. AgentCore-Light has no code-signing certificate and new releases have few downloads, which is exactly what “rather be wrong than sorry” rules pick up. The software runs only on your own machine and sends no data out — see 2.2 What to do when your system says “not safe” for the details and the steps.

#The device does not power on when I hold the button

  • Check that you are holding the button for a full 3 seconds.
  • Charge the device first and try again.
  • Plugging in USB does not power it on; you still need to hold the button.

#Bluetooth mode keeps blinking red

  • Check that Bluetooth is switched on in Windows.
  • The first time, pair AgentCore-Light in Windows Settings first.
  • After pairing, click Connect on the Windows device details page.
  • Then go back to the Bluetooth page in AgentCore-Light and start Bluetooth mode.

#USB mode keeps blinking yellow

  • Check that you have short-pressed the button to switch to USB mode.
  • Swap in a Type-C cable that supports data transfer.
  • Check that you have clicked Start USB mode on the USB serial page in AgentCore-Light.

#The app says it connected, but the light does not change

  • Check that the device shows green breathing.
  • In Light control, test the red, yellow and green lights first.
  • Check that the Brightness slider is not at 0%.
  • Try stopping and restarting the relevant Bluetooth or USB serial mode.

#Can Bluetooth and USB be used at the same time?

No. The device runs one transport at a time; that keeps power draw down and the connection stable. Short-press the button to switch.

#There is dust or a fingerprint on the shell — has it been used?

No. Every unit is assembled and fully function-tested before it leaves the factory (power, light effects, buzzer, button). It is handled on the workbench during testing and packing, so a little dust or a fingerprint on the shell is normal and does not affect anything. One wipe with the alcohol pad in the box clears it; the unit works fine.

#After a while idle the light goes out by itself

Not a fault: after 10 minutes continuously idle the device turns the lights off and sleeps. The connection is not dropped, and the light comes back by itself when your AI tool has new work — no button press needed. See 8.2 Going to sleep after a while idle.

#The device powered itself off

In Bluetooth mode, after 5 minutes with no connection (computer shut down, Bluetooth off, or app quit) the device powers itself off — plugging in USB does not prevent it. Press and hold the button for about 3 seconds to power on again. See 8.3 Auto power-off when Bluetooth drops.

#10. Precautions

  • Do not open the device, short its connectors, or use a power supply that does not match.
  • Avoid using or charging it in damp, hot or flammable surroundings.
  • Use ordinary USB power sources and a cable that meets spec.
  • For firmware updates, repairs or fault-finding, contact support — we do not recommend customers doing this themselves.
